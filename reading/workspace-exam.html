Reading Passage 1

Circadian Rhythms and the Melatonin Cycle

Bản đề thực tế trích từ bộ dữ liệu Cambridge Test 1

Paragraph A: Circadian rhythms are biological oscillations with an endogenous frequency approximating twenty-four hours. These rhythms are coordinated by a primary circadian pacemaker situated within the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) of the anterior hypothalamus in mammalian brains. Exposure to environmental photic stimuli synchronizes the internal cellular clock to astronomical solar cycles, a physiological adaptation enabling terrestrial organisms to anticipate environmental variations.

Paragraph B: Central to neuroendocrine regulation is the hormone melatonin (N-acetyl-5-methoxytryptamine), synthesized predominantly within the pineal gland during periods of darkness. Photic transmission proceeds from retinal ganglion cells via the retinohypothalamic tract to the SCN, which subsequently suppresses norepinephrine-mediated pineal activity. Consequently, serum melatonin concentrations exhibit significant nocturnal elevations, functioning as an internal biochemical indicator of temporal diurnal position.

Paragraph C: Researchers have examined pharmaceutical interventions targeted at circadian desynchrony, such as phase shifts induced by rapid transmeridian travel. Controlled administration of exogenous melatonin facilitates phase advances when ingested prior to habitual bedtime, effectively re-entraining circadian misalignment. However, clinicians emphasize that pharmacotherapy must be precisely scheduled relative to individual phase-response curves, as inappropriate timing may exacerbate existing phase delays.

Paragraph D: The architecture of non-rapid eye movement (NREM) and rapid eye movement (REM) cycles demonstrates progressive homeostatic regulation across nocturnal sleep periods. Slow-wave activity predominates early sleep cycles, serving critical functions in metabolic clearance and neuroplastic synaptic consolidation. Disruption of these sequential phases frequently produces cognitive deficits, impaired sustained attention, and immunological alterations.
